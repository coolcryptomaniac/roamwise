Why India needs a different kind of AI planner

Most "best AI travel planner" lists are written for someone flying from New York to Lisbon. They rank tools on how pretty the map looks and how many hotels they can book. They rarely ask whether the AI knows that Dehradun to Rishikesh takes about an hour by road rather than thirty minutes, that Manali to Spiti is a full day of driving, or that a Char Dham trip needs registration before you set off.

For Indian travellers those details decide whether a plan works. A general model that assumes highway speeds will cheerfully schedule three hill towns in one day. It will not warn you about a landslide-prone stretch in the monsoon, a permit for Adi Kailash, or a Tatkal window that opens at 10:00 for AC classes. Most trips here are also group trips with friends or family, so splitting costs fairly matters as much as the itinerary itself.

So we judged each planner the way an Indian traveller actually plans: road reality first, then the group, then money, then booking.

What to look for in an AI travel planner for India

  • Realistic road times. Mountain and ghat roads often average 20 to 35 km/h. A planner that ignores terrain will overload every day. Before any hill drive, also check Uttarakhand road and safety status and the state's travel rules and permits.
  • Group planning. Polls for dates and places, a shared budget, and a clear record of who paid what. Without this, the group chat becomes the planner.
  • Train support. IRCTC, Tatkal timing and PNR status matter more here than in most countries. Our Tatkal booking guide explains the opening window.
  • Honest prices in rupees. Surprise charges at check-in are a common complaint about online stays. Use our booking scam checker and see what things really cost in India.
  • Language. Hindi and Hinglish support makes planning easier for parents and for travellers outside the metros.
  • Offline and low-signal use. Much of the Himalaya has patchy coverage, so a saved plan or PDF beats a live-only app.

2. MakeMyTrip Myra: best for booking in one chat

Myra is the AI assistant inside MakeMyTrip. It spans flights, accommodation, holidays, ground transport, visas and forex through a network of specialised AI agents. Myra 2.0 adds booking completion, payment included, inside the same conversation, and is rolling out in phases.

Pricing: free inside the MakeMyTrip app.

Pros: a huge inventory, text or voice in Hindi and English, and the fastest path from an idea to a paid booking.

Cons: it is built to sell MakeMyTrip's own inventory, so suggestions lean towards what it can book. It is light on group coordination and cost splitting.

Best for: travellers who roughly know what they want and want it booked quickly.

3. Mindtrip: best all-round planner for international trips

Mindtrip combines a chat assistant with an interactive map and a live itinerary. Several independent 2026 roundups place it first for most international trips.

Pricing: core planning is free; the company earns from bookings rather than subscriptions.

Pros: a polished map-plus-chat workspace that is strong for open-ended discovery.

Cons: you must sign in with Google or Apple before you see an itinerary, and it is not tuned for Indian road conditions.

Best for: international holidays where discovery matters more than local logistics.

4. Layla: best for going from inspiration to booking

Layla plans conversationally, mixes in creator videos and pulls live prices from several booking platforms.

Pricing: a free tier, with Premium at $9.99 a month or $49 a year.

Pros: fun to use, with bucket lists you can share with friends.

Cons: some users report wrong connections and hotel price mismatches, and prices are shown in dollars.

Best for: travellers who want ideas and bookable options in one place.

5. Wanderlog: best for organising a trip you have researched

Wanderlog is an itinerary-and-map organiser with real-time collaboration and expense tracking.

Pricing: free, with Pro at $39.99 a year for offline access, route optimisation and Google Maps export.

Pros: a generous free plan with excellent collaboration.

Cons: offline maps, which you need in the hills, are behind Pro, and for most users Pro is sold yearly only. Some Indian reviewers on the App Store say that feels poor value for one or two trips a year.

Best for: planners who have done the research and want it tidy and shared.

6. ChatGPT: best for brainstorming

ChatGPT is a general assistant rather than a travel product, but many people start trip ideas there.

Pricing: free, with paid plans that add more usage and browsing.

Pros: flexible and quick for questions like "where should we go in March with kids?"

Cons: no live prices, maps or bookings by default. It can invent places and badly underestimate Indian hill roads, so verify every timing.

7. Google Gemini: best if you live in Google Maps

Gemini connects to Google Maps and can save places you find in screenshots.

Pricing: free, with paid Google AI plans.

Pros: handy if your saved places already live in Google's apps.

Cons: travel features are scattered across several Google products, there is no group planning, and it can still invent places.

8. Wonderplan: best free themed itineraries

Wonderplan generates themed trips such as food tours and history routes.

Pricing: core features free, with Premium at $49.99 a year.

Pros: quick themed plans at no cost.

Cons: plans often need manual fixes, multi-city routes are generic, and there is no booking or app.

Prompts that get better Indian itineraries from any AI

Whichever tool you use, the request you type shapes the answer. These habits help:

  1. Give the start point and the real constraint. "Delhi, 4 days, two parents in their sixties, no more than four hours on the road per day" beats "4 days in the hills".
  2. Name the season. July in the Himalaya and December in the Himalaya are different trips. Ask the AI to flag monsoon, snow and festival crowds.
  3. Ask for road time per leg, not just distance. Then compare it with a map app and the local road-status page before you book stays.
  4. Ask what needs advance arrangement. Permits, registrations, Tatkal windows, helicopter slots and festival tickets are where trips usually fail.
  5. Set a total budget in rupees and ask for the split. Stay, transport, food and buffer. A buffer of 10 to 15 percent is a sensible planning choice for hill trips, not a rule.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best AI travel planner for India?

For trips inside India, RoamWise, because it plans with terrain-aware Indian road times, group cost splits and train tools. For booking flights and hotels in one conversation, MakeMyTrip's Myra is the strongest.

Is there a free AI trip planner for India?

Yes. RoamWise, Mindtrip, Wanderlog, ChatGPT, Google Gemini and Wonderplan all have free plans, and Myra is free inside the MakeMyTrip app. RoamWise lets you plan without signing up.

Can an AI travel planner book IRCTC train tickets?

None of the planners in this comparison books IRCTC tickets for you. RoamWise helps a group choose a train, prepare for the Tatkal window and read a PNR, then hands you to IRCTC or ixigo to book.

Is ChatGPT good enough to plan a trip in India?

For ideas, yes. For a day-by-day plan, check every road time and opening hour, because general chatbots often underestimate hill and ghat roads and can invent places.

How do I split trip costs with friends in India?

RoamWise's trip hub records who paid what, works out the fewest transfers needed to settle up and opens a UPI payment for each one. Wanderlog also tracks shared expenses.

Do AI travel planners get Indian road times right?

Most general planners assume highway speeds. Hill roads in Uttarakhand and Himachal often average 20 to 35 km/h, so a 60 km drive can take two to three hours. Use a planner that accounts for terrain and check local road status before you leave.
