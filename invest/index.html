Where should RoamWise incorporate?

Our own analysis, shown so you can attack it

Most founders ask "which platform is best?" That question has no answer, because it leaves out the only thing that matters before revenue: what you don't yet know.

The better question is which option costs least per future it keeps open. On that measure, incorporating in India now and flipping to a Delaware parent only if a US fund actually issues a term sheet costs ₹15,000 per future kept open — against ₹1,10,200 for Stripe Atlas today.

Then we attacked our own answer. Flipping later genuinely costs about ₹8 lakh in lawyer, CA and share-swap work. So carrying a Delaware entity from day one pays off only if the probability of a US venture round exceeds 41%. Our honest estimate for RoamWise over three years is around 5% — travel-tech funding is at a decade low, and we have 4 paying users.

The ₹3.3 lakh is spent with certainty, today, out of ₹400 of revenue. The ₹8 lakh is spent only in the world where we already won — and in that world it is a rounding error on the round itself.

If you think our 5% is wrong, that is exactly the conversation we want to have. Vote above and tell us why.

Where this goes — and who still owns it.

Most decks stop at the ask. Here is the whole path to IPO, and exactly how much gets sold at each step. A company with real numbers earns the right to sell less each round — that's why the percentages fall.

🛡️ Why the founder still holds the majority at IPO

Two deliberate choices. One: we sell a smaller slice each round instead of raising the biggest cheque available — the discipline compounds. Two: at Series A we adopt dual-class shares, where founder shares carry 10 votes each. It's how Meta, Alphabet and most modern founder-led companies stay founder-led.

At IPO that's ~54% economic ownership and ~92% of the votes. Plain English: RoamWise cannot be captured, sold out from under it, or steered by a rival buying in — unless the founder decides to sell.

That protects you too. The reason you'd back a solo founder is that he keeps building. This is what makes sure he still can in year ten.

These are projections, not promises. Real rounds move on real numbers, and most companies never reach Series C. Shown so you can see the intent and argue with the assumptions.

The gap, stated plainly

WeRoad matches a 28-year-old to a 28-year-old and calls it a match. But a 28-year-old who wants 6am starts and a 28-year-old who wants 3am nights will have a miserable week — and WeRoad's own model leaves 30–40% of the itinerary for the group to decide, while giving them no tool to decide it with.

We match on the six things groups actually argue about — wake-up time, pace, spend, plan vs improvise, social energy, comfort floor — and then hand the group the tool to settle the other 40%: vote, split to the paise, settle on UPI.

On our engine, those two 28-year-olds score 27%. Age-matching calls them perfect.

We don't compete for the same trip

WeRoad sells you a trip. We make the trip you were already taking work. Most group travel is friends, not strangers — and nobody serves that.

We're the layer under both

A Hosteller guest still has to split the bill. A WeRoad group still has to decide dinner. Both are customers, not rivals.
