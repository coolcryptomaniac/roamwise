01 — The problem

Travellers don't lack inspiration.
They lack local knowledge.

Every AI trip planner produces the same output: a confident list of the same six places, written in the same voice, with "budget-friendly" where a number should be.

💸

No real prices

"Affordable" is not a number. Travellers get quoted 3× the local rate because they have no idea what the rate is.

🕳️

Confident hallucination

LLM planners routinely invent restaurants and recommend places that closed years ago.

📵

Useless offline

The moment you most need your itinerary is usually the moment you have no data.

🌍

Western-centric

Built for a traveller flying into Rome, not one taking a sleeper from Delhi.

02 — Insight

The gap is not intelligence.
It's ground truth.

Anyone can generate a Goa itinerary. Almost nobody can tell you that the auto to Anjuna should cost ₹180, that "your hotel is closed" is a commission scam, and then show you all of it with your phone in airplane mode.

Generic travel advice is now free and infinite. What remains scarce — and defensible — is verified local economic knowledge. That is a data moat, not a model moat, and it compounds.

05 — Why the moat holds

Public data is the floor.
Verified local ground-truth is the moat.

The fair question every investor asks: if the base data is on the internet, what stops a competitor copying it? The base layer is copyable. The layer that makes RoamWise useful is not.

Layer 1 — Public base (copyable, and that's fine)

OpenStreetMap, Wikivoyage, government transit data. Anyone can use these — they give structure, not truth. They cannot tell you the auto from Rishikesh station to Ram Jhula actually costs ₹150, not the ₹400 a tourist is quoted.

Layer 2 — Verified ground-truth (the proprietary asset)

Real local cost bands, scam patterns and seasonal pricing, each verified by a paid contributor on the ground and stamped with who checked it and when. This does not exist on the internet to scrape — we create it.

How it stays current — and compounds

Contributors re-verify on a rolling cycle; users flag stale prices, which routes back to re-verification. Every month live, the dataset gets more accurate. A copyist starts from zero on launch day — and is a year behind by the time they match our base.

Why a giant won't just do it

A global planner optimises for 190 countries and cannot justify paying people to price-check autos in 40 Indian cities. The narrowness that looks like a limitation is exactly what makes the asset defensible.

The model is rentable and the base data is public — but the verified local layer is built by us, owned by us, and harder to catch every month it runs.
