A travel budget that survives taxes, transfers and shared costs

A reusable India trip-budget worksheet with complete-cost comparisons and clear labels for quotes, estimates and actual spending.

By RoamWise Editorial · Published and checked 2026-09-10

A useful travel budget is a record of assumptions. It does not claim what every local pays. Prices vary by city, season, product, booking time and traveller needs. The former version of this page published broad “local versus tourist” tables as verified facts without a supporting survey. They have been removed. This replacement shows how to build and update your own total from comparable quotes.

Give every number a label

Four labels that prevent false certainty
LabelMeaningExample note
QuoteA supplier offered this exact service at a recorded time.Room category, dates, taxes, meals, cancellation and quote time.
EstimateYour current planning allowance.“₹800 food per day; replace after choosing restaurants.”
Fixed ruleA fare or fee supported by a current official source.Save its URL and checked date; confirm it applies to your class and date.
ActualWhat you paid after the trip.Include fees and refunds so the comparison remains honest.

A price seen on one app is a quote from that channel, not a market average. A friend’s payment is an old observation, not your fare. Keep useful evidence without turning it into a universal statement.

Compare the same product

For rooms, match dates, occupancy, room category, tax, breakfast and cancellation rights. For transport, match departure point, arrival point, baggage and flexibility. For an activity, match duration, transfers, equipment and cancellation conditions. A lower headline price may describe a smaller or riskier purchase.

Suppose Hotel A is ₹1,800 plus ₹500 in taxes and breakfast is separate. Hotel B is ₹2,300 including tax and breakfast. If you planned ₹350 for breakfast, A totals ₹2,650 and B totals ₹2,300. This is invented arithmetic, not a hotel recommendation. It shows why the checkout total and inclusions matter.

Create six budget rows

  1. Reach the destination: fare, baggage, booking fee and travel to the departure point.
  2. Sleep: number of charged nights, taxes, early access and deposits that temporarily reduce available cash.
  3. Move locally: airport or station transfer, daily transport, fuel, charging, tolls and parking.
  4. Eat and drink: a daily allowance plus any meal that is part of the experience.
  5. Do: admissions, guides, activities, permits and equipment.
  6. Protect the plan: insurance if chosen, medicine, a contingency and the cost of getting home if one component fails.

Record shared expenses once. If two people share a ₹3,000 room, the trip total contains ₹3,000 and the per-person share is ₹1,500. Do not add both figures. A refundable deposit affects available cash even if it is not expected to be a final cost, so list it separately.

None of these amounts claims a current Indian average. Copy the structure and replace every input. Keep contingency outside the amount you plan to spend; it exists for changes, not as a target.

Update the budget at decision points

Recalculate after booking intercity transport, after choosing a stay and shortly before departure. Record refund deadlines next to each prepaid item. If one row rises, choose which flexible row changes; do not hope the difference disappears. For a route comparison, use the door-to-door guide.

Keep payment confirmation and supplier identity. When bank details change, verify through an official contact. A spreadsheet cannot protect you from a false payee, and a low quote is not evidence that the seller can deliver the service.

Make the next trip smarter

After returning, replace estimates with actuals and add one sentence explaining large differences. “Taxi rose by ₹600 because the arrival point changed” is more useful than declaring a city expensive. Aggregate only like-for-like trips, with dates and circumstances preserved. This is how RoamWise intends to earn trust: clear inputs, reproducible arithmetic and honest limits.
