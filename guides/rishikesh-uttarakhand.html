Rishikesh: what it actually costs, and what the maps get wrong

Most Rishikesh guides tell you it's the yoga capital of the world. This one tells you what an auto from the station should cost, why your drive will take longer than Google says, and which months are genuinely worth avoiding.

By the RoamWise team · Almora, Uttarakhand · Updated August 2026 · Prices verified on the ground

The first thing that will cost you money

You will arrive at Rishikesh railway station, or be dropped at the ISBT bus stand, and an auto driver will quote you ₹400 to Laxman Jhula. The locals standing behind you are paying ₹150. Neither of you is wrong about the destination — you are just being quoted the tourist rate, and it is entirely legal for him to try.

The shared autos that run the Ram Jhula–Laxman Jhula–Tapovan route charge ₹20 to ₹30 a person. They leave when full, which in season means a two-minute wait. If you are carrying a large bag and want the whole vehicle, ₹150 to ₹200 is the honest private rate from the station to Laxman Jhula, and ₹250 if you are going up to Tapovan.

The number that matters: say the price you expect before you get in. "Laxman Jhula, ₹150?" gets a yes or a counter. Getting in first and asking after means you have already lost the negotiation.

What we verified in August 2026: Station to Ram Jhula ₹120–150 private, ₹20 shared. Station to Laxman Jhula ₹150–200 private, ₹30 shared. Station to Tapovan ₹200–250. Haridwar station to Rishikesh ₹600–800 by private cab, ₹60 by bus. Rates rise about 30% after 9pm and during Char Dham season.

Getting there: the drive times nobody adjusts for

Google Maps will tell you Delhi to Rishikesh is five and a half hours. It is, at 4am on a Tuesday in November. Any other time, budget seven to eight — and during Char Dham season (May to June), nine is realistic.

The reason is not distance. It is that the last 40km, from Haridwar onward, is a two-lane road shared with pilgrimage traffic, and the stretch through Haridwar itself can add an hour on its own during festival weeks.

That last row is the one that catches people out. Mountain roads in Uttarakhand run at roughly 22 to 32 km/h in practice, not the 50 km/h a flat-road estimate assumes. A 200km Himalayan drive is a full day, not an afternoon.

Do not plan a same-day return to anywhere past Devprayag. People try it, get caught by a landslide clearance or an evening roadblock, and end up sleeping in the car on a hill road. Book the night somewhere and drive back in daylight.

Rafting: the part where safety actually matters

Rishikesh rafting is genuinely excellent and genuinely dangerous when done badly. The Ganga here runs Grade II to Grade IV depending on the stretch and the season.

What the stretches mean

  • Brahmapuri to Rishikesh (9km) — Grade I–II. Fine for children over 12 and complete beginners. About ₹500–700.
  • Shivpuri to Rishikesh (16km) — Grade II–III, includes Roller Coaster and Golf Course rapids. The standard choice. ₹900–1,200.
  • Marine Drive to Rishikesh (25km) — Grade III–IV. You should have rafted before. ₹1,500–2,000.
  • Kaudiyala to Rishikesh (36km) — Grade IV. Experienced only. ₹2,000–2,500.

How to tell a real operator from a booth

Rishikesh has hundreds of rafting booths and considerably fewer licensed operators. The ones that matter:

  • Ask if they are registered with the Uttarakhand Tourism Development Board. A real operator says yes immediately and can show the certificate.
  • ATOAI membership (Adventure Tour Operators Association of India) is a strong signal. It is voluntary and takes effort to get.
  • Look at the helmets and life jackets before you pay. Cracked helmets and faded jackets mean equipment that has not been replaced in years.
  • A guide who does not do a safety briefing on the bank is a guide you should not get in a boat with. Walk away; you will lose ₹1,000 and possibly save more.
Rafting is closed from roughly late June to mid-September. Monsoon flow makes the river genuinely unsafe, and operators who offer it anyway during those months are telling you everything you need to know about their judgement.

Things worth knowing before you arrive

  • Rishikesh is dry and vegetarian by law. No alcohol, no meat, within the municipal area. This surprises people every single day.
  • The Ganga Aarti at Parmarth Niketan is free and starts around sunset. The one at Triveni Ghat is larger and more local. Both are worth going to; neither requires a ticket, and anyone selling you one is not selling you anything real.
  • The Beatles Ashram (Chaurasi Kutia) charges ₹150 for Indians and ₹600 for foreigners. Go in the morning; the light through the graffiti is better and the crowds arrive after 11.
  • Cash still matters. UPI works nearly everywhere in town, but shared autos, small chai stalls and the Triund-side vendors often want notes. Carry ₹500 in small denominations.
  • The two bridges — Ram Jhula and Laxman Jhula — are pedestrian only, and Laxman Jhula has been closed to traffic and occasionally to pedestrians for structural work. Check locally before planning to cross.

Where to base yourself

Tapovan

Above Laxman Jhula. Where most travellers stay: cafés, yoga schools, guesthouses at every price. Lively, walkable, slightly removed from the ghats. Best default choice for a first visit.

Ram Jhula / Swarg Ashram

Closer to the ashrams and the aarti. Quieter in the evening, more pilgrims than backpackers, generally cheaper. Better if you came for the spiritual side rather than the cafés.

Upstream (Shivpuri, Byasi)

Riverside camps 15–30km up the road. Beautiful, and you will need transport for everything. Good for two nights as part of a rafting trip, less good as a whole-week base.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Rishikesh?

Three full days covers the aarti, one rafting run, the Beatles Ashram and a day walking the ghats without rushing. Five to seven if you are doing a yoga course or using it as a base for a Himalayan onward trip.

Is Rishikesh safe for solo female travellers?

Broadly yes, and it is one of the more comfortable Indian towns for it — large international presence, walkable, plenty of women travelling alone. The usual caution applies after dark on the quieter stretches between Ram Jhula and Tapovan, where the road is unlit. Shared autos stop running around 9pm.

What should an auto from Rishikesh station cost?

₹150–200 private to Laxman Jhula, ₹20–30 shared. If you are quoted ₹400, that is the tourist rate and a counter of ₹180 is usually accepted.

Can you drink alcohol in Rishikesh?

No. Rishikesh is a legally dry city and meat is not served within municipal limits. The nearest places serving alcohol are outside the municipal boundary, towards Haridwar.

Is rafting available all year?

No. Rafting typically runs late September to late June and closes during the monsoon when the flow becomes unsafe. Any operator selling monsoon rafting is one to avoid.
