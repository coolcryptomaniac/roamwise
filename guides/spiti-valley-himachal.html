Spiti Valley: the drive times are three times longer than you think

Almost every Spiti itinerary we see online is dangerously optimistic. Not because the places are wrong — because a 200km day in Spiti takes nine hours, not three, and the altitude does not care about your schedule.

By the RoamWise team · Almora, Uttarakhand · Updated August 2026 · Road times from our terrain model

The single most important number

Himalayan roads in Spiti run at roughly 22 km/h in real conditions. Not 50. Not the 60 a mapping app assumes when it sees a road classified as a highway.

This is not pessimism. It is the combination of single-lane sections, unpaved stretches after water crossings, hairpins that require a full stop to negotiate, oncoming trucks that require reversing to a passing point, and the simple fact that at 4,000m your vehicle makes less power.

The itinerary that gets people hurt: "Manali to Kaza in a day, Chandratal the next." Manali–Kaza alone is a full day that starts before dawn. Adding Chandratal means arriving at 4,300m having gained 2,300m in 36 hours. That is how altitude sickness becomes a medical emergency in a place with one hospital.

Altitude: the part that is not optional

Kaza sits at 3,800m. Chandratal is 4,300m. Kunzum La is 4,590m. At these heights roughly one traveller in four experiences some form of acute mountain sickness, and the difference between a headache and an evacuation is almost entirely about how fast you climbed.

The schedule that works

  1. Enter via Shimla and Kinnaur, not Manali. The Kinnaur route gains altitude over two to three days instead of one. It is longer and it is the reason experienced travellers use it.
  2. Sleep two nights below 3,500m before going higher. Kalpa (2,960m) or Nako (3,662m) are the natural stops.
  3. Spend your first full day in Kaza doing nothing strenuous. Walk the market. Do not drive to Langza on day one.
  4. Climb high, sleep low where possible. Day-trip to Komic or Hikkim, come back down to Kaza to sleep.

What to watch for

Headache that does not respond to water and paracetamol, nausea, breathlessness at rest, confusion, or a dry persistent cough. Any of these mean descend — not rest, not wait, descend. Losing 500m of altitude does more than any medicine available locally.

The nearest real hospital is in Reckong Peo or Manali, both a full day's drive from Kaza. Kaza's community health centre handles basics. Plan as though serious help is a day away, because it is.

Where to actually go

Key Monastery

The image you have seen of Spiti is probably this. A thousand-year-old Gelugpa monastery stacked on a hill above the Spiti river, 14km from Kaza. Go at dawn — the light on the valley is the reason, and you will have it nearly to yourself. Monks serve butter tea to visitors; take it, and leave a donation rather than a payment.

Langza, Komic and Hikkim

A cluster above Kaza at around 4,400m. Langza has the large Buddha statue and marine fossils in the surrounding fields — genuinely, this was seabed. Komic claims to be among the highest villages with a motorable road. Hikkim has the world's highest post office, and a postcard sent from it takes a fortnight and is worth every day.

Chandratal

A crescent lake at 4,300m, accessible only in summer, and the single most demanding stop on most itineraries. Camping there is spectacular and it is also 4,300m — do not sleep at Chandratal until you have already spent several nights above 3,500m.

Dhankar

A monastery on a spur between two valleys, with a lake an hour's walk above it. Less visited than Key and, to our eye, the more dramatic setting.

Practical things nobody mentions

  • There is very little mobile signal. BSNL works patchily in Kaza; most other networks do not work at all in the valley. Download offline maps and tell someone your route before you go.
  • ATMs are unreliable. Kaza has one or two and they run out. Carry enough cash for the whole trip — homestays are ₹600–800 a night and almost all are cash only.
  • Nights are cold even in July. 5°C at night in midsummer is normal. Most homestays have thick blankets and no heating.
  • The food is limited and that is fine. Dal, rice, thukpa, momos, and a great deal of butter tea. Vegan travellers manage easily; anyone needing variety will not find it.
  • Carry your rubbish out. Spiti has almost no waste infrastructure. What you bring in either leaves with you or stays in the valley forever.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do you need for Spiti?

Seven minimum from Manali if you value acclimatisation. Ten if entering through Kinnaur, which is the safer route. Anything under five days is either exhausting or a health risk.

Can you drive to Spiti in an ordinary car?

Via Kinnaur, yes, in a high-clearance hatchback driven carefully. Via Manali and Kunzum La, you want an SUV — there are water crossings that will strand a low car.

Is Spiti safe for beginners at altitude?

Yes, if you go in via Kinnaur and follow an acclimatisation schedule. It is not safe if you drive Manali to Kaza in one day, which is what most rushed itineraries do.

Do you need a permit for Spiti?

Indian nationals need no permit for the main valley. Foreign nationals need an Inner Line Permit for the stretch between Rekong Peo and Kaza, obtainable at Rekong Peo or Kaza. Rules change — verify before travelling.

Is there an EV charging network in Spiti?

No. Charging effectively stops at Reckong Peo. Any "electric Spiti trip" you are offered is not accurate for the high sections.
