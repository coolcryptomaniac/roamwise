What things should cost in India

Guide · updated July 2026 · roughly a 9 minute read

Almost every unpleasant money moment in Indian travel comes from the same gap: you do not know what the thing costs, and the person quoting you does. Close that gap and most of the problem disappears. You do not need to haggle well. You need to know the number.

What follows are the bands I use in the RoamWise cost engine. They are modelled from published fare structures and typical rates rather than any single city, so treat them as the shape of reality rather than a quotation. A metro city sits at the top of each band, a small town near the bottom.

Getting around a city

ModeTypical rateWhat to know

App cab (Uber/Ola)₹25–35/km + surgeAlmost always cheaper than a street taxi at a tourist spot.
Bike taxi (Rapido)₹10–15/kmCheapest for one person in traffic. A helmet should be provided.
Scooter rental₹400–700/dayPhotograph every existing scratch before riding away.
City bus₹10–30 per tripSlow, honest pricing, and a genuinely good way to see a city.
Metro₹10–60Where it exists it beats everything on both time and cost.

The single most useful habit

Ask your hotel what a fair fare is before you step outside. It takes one minute, they know exactly, and it removes most overcharging for the rest of your stay. If they say ₹150 and the driver says ₹500, you now have a number to hold rather than a feeling that something is wrong.

Where to sleep

  • Hostel dorm: ₹400–900
  • Budget room: ₹800–1,800
  • Mid-range hotel: ₹2,000–4,500
  • Premium: ₹6,000 and up

Homestays frequently beat hotels in hill regions on both price and quality, and the money stays with a family rather than a chain. Book direct where you can — aggregator commission is typically 15–20% and the family absorbs it.

Eating

  • Street plate: ₹30–80
  • Dhaba thali: ₹100–200 — usually the best value meal in the country
  • Cafe meal: ₹250–500
  • Restaurant dinner: ₹600–1,200

The rule that never fails: eat where there is a queue of locals. High turnover means fresh food, and popularity among people who have other options is the only review that matters.

The specific tricks, and how they work

These are pricing and paperwork tricks — checkable, mechanical things. Knowing the mechanism is what disarms them.

  • "Your hotel is closed / full / burned down." It has not. This is a commission run to a place that pays the driver. Call your hotel and hand the driver the phone.
  • The broken meter. Agree a price out loud before moving, or use an app. Never "we will see at the end".
  • The free thing. A bracelet tied on your wrist, a blessing, henna on your hand. Nothing offered unprompted is free.
  • The scooter damage claim. Film a slow walk-around before you ride off, including the number plate in the same shot.
  • The festival-only shop. "Everything else is closed today because of a festival" is almost never true.
  • Change confusion. Count it before walking away. Carry ₹10, ₹20 and ₹50 notes so exact fares are possible.

If a quote feels wrong

Say the number you expected, calmly, and be willing to walk. In most Indian cities another auto arrives within a minute. Walking away is not rudeness — it is the normal functioning of the market, and both of you know it.

Budgeting a whole trip

The costs people forget are the ones that break budgets: airport transfers at both ends, daily local transit, tips, the SIM card, and the buffer for the thing that goes wrong. Add roughly 10% on top of your calculated total and you will rarely be caught out.
