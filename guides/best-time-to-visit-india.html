When to visit India

Guide · updated July 2026 · roughly a 7 minute read

India is not one climate, which is why "the best time to visit India" is a question with no answer. There is a good time for the Himalaya, a good time for Rajasthan and a good time for Kerala, and they are not the same months.

Timing is also the cheapest lever you have. The same room in Manali can cost three times more in late December than in early November, and the town is far pleasanter in November. Most of the difference between a good trip and a frustrating one is chosen at the booking stage.

By season

October to March — the broad sweet spot for most of India

Post-monsoon skies, comfortable temperatures across the plains, and the peak of the festival calendar. Rajasthan, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Kerala and the coasts are all at their best. This is also when everyone else visits, so book earlier than feels necessary and start sightseeing at opening time.

April to June — hot on the plains, perfect in the mountains

Northern plains reach 45°C and sightseeing becomes genuinely unpleasant. This is exactly when the Himalaya opens up: Ladakh becomes accessible, Spiti's passes clear, and Himachal and Uttarakhand are at their most beautiful. Hill stations are busy with domestic travellers escaping the same heat.

July to September — monsoon

Widely avoided and frequently misjudged. The Western Ghats, Meghalaya and Goa's interior are spectacular under rain, prices drop sharply and crowds vanish. The genuine cautions: landslides close hill roads with little warning, and trekking in the high Himalaya is a poor idea. Spiti and Ladakh sit in a rain shadow and stay largely dry, which makes them the exception.

The crowd trick that works everywhere

The shoulder weeks either side of peak season give you nearly the same weather at a fraction of the price and crowd. Early October and late March are the two most underrated windows in Indian travel.

Within a day, the same logic applies at smaller scale: major monuments are calm for the first ninety minutes after opening and busy from mid-morning. Being at the gate when it opens changes the experience more than any other single decision.

Festivals — plan around, not into

Festivals are extraordinary to witness and brutal to travel during. Trains sell out weeks ahead around Diwali, Holi and Durga Puja, prices spike, and roads clog. Arrive before the rush, stay put through it, and travel after.

Worth building a trip around: Pushkar Camel Fair (November), Hornbill Festival in Nagaland (December), Rann Utsav in Gujarat (winter), Ziro Music Festival in Arunachal (September).
