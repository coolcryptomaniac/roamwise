A practical approach plan

  1. Get the portfolio ready. Prepare 3–6 actual artwork photos, artist bio, medium, original/print/digital format, dimensions and units, delivered prices, lead time and proof of authorship. Get rights to display each photo.
  2. Choose one relevant use. A reception piece, two guest-room landscapes, a devotional home painting or a gift order. Check the wall, light, humidity, framing and maintenance needs.
  3. Contact the right person. Property: owner/GM, designer or procurement for purchases; guest-experience manager for workshops. Gallery: curator/advisor. Home buyer: the artist directly through their profile. Ask permission before sending large attachments.
  4. Send a short proposal. “I’m [name], an artist working in [style]. I suggest [specific piece/use] for [space]. Here are three examples and prices. May I speak with the person responsible for art or interiors?” Only claim affiliations you actually have.
  5. Agree everything in writing. Scope, item count, size, materials, reproduction rights, framing, delivery, installation, cancellations, returns and payment milestones. For consignment, also record inventory, commission, damage responsibility and payout dates.
  6. Follow up once. After 5–7 working days, send one polite follow-up. Record the reply; stop if declined. Do not mark a prospect as a partner until both sides agree.
